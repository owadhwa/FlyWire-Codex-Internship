The largest circuit motif identity consists of 11 nodes, with neurons
that are both GABAergic and serotonergic and that span the hemispheres
of the fly brain (Figure 1). These local neurons lack the traditional
separation of dendritic and axonal arborizations and contain only
dendritic arborizations. These neurons can be visualized in Figure 2.

1.  **GABAergic local neurons shape odor tuning and contrast.**
    GABAergic inhibition in the Drosophila antennal lobe shapes
    odor-evoked spatiotemporal activity patterns and can regulate
    projection neuron responses, helping transform glomerular input into
    more selective output patterns<sup>1</sup>.

2.  **Dense LN–LN connectivity suggests recurrent normalization/gain
    control.** The graph shows many reciprocal or near-reciprocal
    connections among LNs, which is consistent with antennal-lobe
    circuits acting as recurrent inhibitory networks rather than simple
    feedforward filters. Prior work shows that *Drosophila*
    antennal-lobe LNs are diverse in morphology, neurotransmitter
    identity, wiring, and physiology, supporting specialized roles in
    odor processing<sup>2</sup>.

**This circuit may coordinate interglomerular inhibition.** LNs in the
antennal lobe connect across glomeruli and can mediate lateral
inhibition, which helps decorrelate odor representations, suppress
broadly activated channels, and improve odor
discrimination<sup>1,3</sup>.

3.  **The serotonergic node may provide state-dependent modulation.**
    The node labeled "serotonergic" is interesting because serotonin has
    been shown to modulate *Drosophila* antennal lobe processing in an
    odor-specific manner, enhancing projection neuron sensitivity and
    influencing inhibitory local interneuron activity<sup>4</sup>.

4.  **The bilateral elements may coordinate odor processing across
    hemispheres.** The circuit includes left and right local neurons,
    suggesting possible cross-hemisphere coupling<sup>5</sup>.
    Biologically, this could help compare bilateral olfactory input,
    stabilize odor representations, or coordinate antennal lobe gain
    across sides, although that specific function would need to be
    tested.
